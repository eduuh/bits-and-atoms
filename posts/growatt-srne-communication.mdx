I went looking for a single write-up pairing a **Growatt SPF** inverter with **SRNE** LiFePO4 batteries over a BMS link, and found nothing. Plenty on Pylontech, Deye, Victron. Nothing on this exact combo. So here's what actually worked, details first.

## The short version

<Callout type="tip" title="Working configuration">
  Everything below is what got a real state-of-charge reading onto the inverter.
</Callout>

| | |
|---|---|
| Inverter | Growatt SPF 3000TL HVM-24 |
| Batteries | 2 × SRNE SR-EOS02B-24 (LiFePO4, 5.12 kWh total) |
| Bus | **CAN** |
| Cable | RJ45, **pins 4 & 5 only** (the blue pair), straight-through |
| Program 05 (battery type) | `LI` |
| Program 36 (protocol) | `52` (CAN) |
| Result | Live SOC % on the inverter, BMS-governed charging |

If you only take one thing: **two wires (pins 4 and 5) and two menu settings.** The rest of this post is why.

## Why bother

Out of the box the inverter runs a generic battery profile and estimates state of charge (SOC) from terminal voltage. On LiFePO4 that's close to useless. The discharge curve is flat, so ~25.6 V could mean 30% or 80% full. Wiring the BMS link gets you two things:

- The **real** SOC, reported by the battery's own BMS.
- **Charge control handed to the BMS**, instead of hand-typed voltage set-points.

## The cable: pins 4 & 5

The comms port is a standard RJ45 jack (same connector as Ethernet), but only two pins matter for CAN. Battery and inverter line up one-to-one:

| Pin | Battery port | Growatt BMS port | For CAN |
|----:|--------------|------------------|---------|
| 1 | RS485-B | RS485-B | none |
| 2 | RS485-A | RS485-A | none |
| 4 | CAN-H | CAN-H | **keep** |
| 5 | CAN-L | CAN-L | **keep** |

Wire it **straight-through**. Growatt pin 4 → battery pin 4, pin 5 → pin 5. And do **not** swap CAN-H and CAN-L. I cut a spare cable down to just the blue pair, left the far end as a normal plug, and checked continuity with a multimeter before plugging in.

<Callout type="warning" title="Ignore the 'cut pins 1 & 2' note">
  The SRNE battery manual says to cut pins 1 & 2 if you use a standard network cable. That instruction is for connecting to an **SRNE-brand inverter** (which runs RS485 on pins 7 & 8). A **Growatt** uses RS485 on pins 1 & 2 and CAN on pins 4 & 5, so cutting 1 & 2 would remove wires you might need. For CAN it doesn't matter either way, but don't follow it blindly.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The blue pair is standard-proof">
  Pins 4 and 5 are the **blue pair**, solid blue (CAN-H) and white/blue (CAN-L), sitting dead center in the connector. Ethernet's two wiring standards (T568A/T568B) only swap the orange and green pairs; **the blue pair never moves**. So for CAN you can ignore which standard your cable is: grab the two middle wires.
</Callout>

## The settings

Two programs on the inverter's LCD:

```
Program 05  →  LI      (battery type: lithium / BMS mode)
Program 36  →  52       (CAN protocol)
```

Setting **Program 05 = `LI`** changes the inverter's behaviour: the max-charge-current setting locks (the BMS owns it now) and the charge/discharge limits switch from volts to a **percentage**. That percentage is your proof the link is live.

**Program 36** picks the protocol. Growatt groups them by bus: RS485 in the **1 to 50** range, CAN in **51 to 99**. SRNE's LFP packs speak Pylontech-style CAN, which lands on **52**. Neither manual prints the exact number. Both say "ask the battery maker". 52 is the one that worked.

## Confirming it worked

The link is up when **all** of these are true:

- The inverter shows a **live SOC %** that tracks the battery.
- Programs 12/13 read in **%**, not volts.
- No BMS/comms fault, and the inverter doesn't cut output.

If CAN won't link, step through the other CAN numbers (51 to 99), then switch to an RS485 cable on **pins 1 & 2** and try the 1 to 50 range.

## Two batteries, one voice

I'm running two SRNE packs in parallel. Only one connects to the inverter:

- **Battery B** `BAT-OUT` → **Battery A** `BAT-IN` (daisy-chain, RJ45).
- **Battery A** `RS485/CAN` → **inverter BMS port** (the pins-4-&-5 cable above).
- DC power from both packs meets at a busbar, each with its own breaker.

Once comms are up, the two report to the inverter as a single 5.12 kWh pack with one SOC.
